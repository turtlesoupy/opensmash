# Special-attack design principles — v4

Describe what the player should recognize, feel, and understand. These principles
apply to any character and do not prescribe a renderer or geometry format.

Propose the complete special-move set together: neutral, up, and down, each with
intentional ground and air behavior. Design the moves in relation to each other,
while making each individual move understandable and satisfying on its own.

1. **One readable idea.** Build each special around a recognizable character
   reference and a dominant silhouette, with a few distinctive details.
2. **Perform the action.** The body and prop anticipate, act decisively, follow
   through, and recover together. Multiple beats should be visibly distinct and
   build toward a stronger finish.
3. **Respect materials and construction.** Objects should look and move like what
   they represent. Rigid parts retain their shape; flexible parts bend or stretch.
   Distinctive structural details remain recognizable throughout the action.
4. **Design for the match camera.** The character, action, and important details
   should read at normal gameplay size, including on a phone. Effects support the
   action without obscuring the fighter.
5. **Choose effects that serve the action.** Body motion, weapons,
   transformations, trails, and particles are optional ways to express a move.
   Use whichever make its identity and danger clearest. A clean physical attack
   can stand alone. Avoid giving every move the same effects structure. When
   fragments are used, their shapes, origins, motion, and disappearance belong
   to the action and remain readable.
6. **Show honest danger.** Strong active effects communicate where and when an
   attack can hit. Contact effects require real contact. Decorative effects remain
   visually subordinate. Preserve readable counterplay.
7. **Preserve identity across contexts.** Ground and air versions share an idea,
   with deliberate stance, timing, and motion differences. Aerial spectacle fits
   its usable duration.
8. **Make a cohesive set with distinct moves.** Share a recognizable visual
   identity across the set, while giving the player meaningfully different
   actions and options. Distribute character references thoughtfully; avoid
   repeating the same attack with a different direction or color. Each move
   should stand on its own when reviewed individually.
