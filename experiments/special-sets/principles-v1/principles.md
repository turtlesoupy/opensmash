# Special-attack principles — v1

The output should communicate the character, the action, and the danger at normal
match-camera size. Apply these principles to any character; invent the concrete
geometry and choreography from their description.

1. **One readable idea.** Build each special around a recognizable character
   reference and a dominant silhouette. Use a few distinctive construction details
   rather than an anonymous block or unrelated decorative noise.
2. **Perform the action.** The body and prop should anticipate, act decisively,
   follow through, and recover together. Match deformation and emission to each
   beat. A multi-beat description needs distinct visible beats and a stronger finish.
3. **Respect material and construction.** Rigid components keep their shape and
   internal spacing; flexible material deforms between its attachments. Separate
   silhouette, structural regions, and surface detail. Leave actual gaps between
   repeated details; an opaque foreground row must not cover the construction it
   describes. For an unrotated repeated stripe, its full width is twice its half-size.
4. **Design for the match camera.** Prioritize silhouette, contrasting structural
   regions, then fine detail. Main forms need a dark boundary and bright readable
   accents. Details that shrink to hairlines should become thicker or simpler,
   including on a phone-sized view. Use depth deliberately so important effects
   appear in front of the body instead of inside it.
5. **Make particles part of the action.** Choose a recognizable, connected fragment
   shape that belongs to this move. Give it enough thickness and contrast to read
   against busy scenery. Emit where the action occurs, let fragments depart and
   fall independently, then fade. Sparse should mean intentional, not invisible.
6. **Show honest danger.** Strong active cues follow the real hit region and its
   lifetime. Decorative trails may linger more quietly. Never seek the opponent
   or draw a contact explosion without real contact. Keep the fighter readable.
7. **Preserve identity across contexts.** Ground and air versions share an idea,
   with deliberate stance, timing and motion differences. Neutral, recovery and
   down specials have distinct roles. Aerial spectacle must fit its usable duration.

These are generation instructions, not an automatic score or judge. The human
reviews the resulting moves and changes these shared principles for the next run.
Do not repair, rank, reroll or hand-polish an individual output to improve a result.
